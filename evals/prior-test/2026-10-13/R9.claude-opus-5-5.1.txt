Key concepts associated with "Reboot" in software development:

**System / Operational**
- **Restart**: shutting down and starting a system, service, or process again
- **Cold vs. warm boot**: full power cycle vs. software-initiated restart
- **State reset**: clearing memory, caches, and transient state to recover from errors
- **Bootloader / boot sequence**: firmware (BIOS/UEFI), kernel loading, init systems (systemd, launchd)
- **Graceful shutdown**: draining connections and flushing data before restarting
- **Uptime and downtime**: availability impact, maintenance windows
- **Rolling restarts**: restarting nodes in a cluster one at a time to avoid outages
- **Crash recovery and watchdogs**: automatic restarts after failures (supervisors, Kubernetes liveness probes)
- **Persistence**: what survives a reboot (disk, config, durable queues) vs. what doesn't (RAM, ephemeral containers)
- **Kernel patching**: updates that require a reboot vs. live patching
- **"Have you tried turning it off and on again?"**: the classic first troubleshooting step

**Project / Product**
- **Rewrite from scratch**: replacing a legacy codebase rather than refactoring it
- **Second-system effect**: the risk of over-engineering a replacement
- **Technical debt reset**: starting fresh to escape accumulated cruft
- **Re-architecture**: rethinking the core design, such as moving from a monolith to microservices
- **Project relaunch**: restarting a stalled or failed initiative with a new scope, team, or approach
- **Brand or product reboot**: reimagining an existing product for new users or markets

**Design Principles**
- **Idempotency**: systems that behave correctly when restarted mid-operation
- **Stateless services**: designed so restarts are cheap and safe
- **Immutable infrastructure**: replace rather than repair ("cattle, not pets")
- **Fail-fast / crash-only software**: designs where restarting is the normal recovery path

**Other**
- **Reboot (framework)**: an open-source framework for building durable, transactional distributed applications
- **Hot reload vs. full restart**: in development tooling, applying changes without restarting vs. needing a full restart
